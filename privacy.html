NFC Canvas privacy policy

Effective November 12, 2026.

NFC Canvas is an Android app that reads and writes NFC tags and creates and scans QR codes. This policy explains what data the app handles, why, and for how long.

What stays on your phone

  • The content of tags and QR codes you read, create or save (links, Wi-Fi networks and passwords, contacts, text). It stays in the app's storage on your device and is never sent anywhere.
  • Camera: used only while you scan a QR code. The image is processed on the device and is neither stored nor sent.
  • NFC: reading and writing happen between the phone and the tag, with no server.
  • Gallery images: when you pick an image to read a QR code, it is read on the device and is neither copied nor sent.
  • Read history (off by default): if you turn it on, the tags and QR codes you read are kept on the device, outside the backup, until you clear the history.
  • Routines and webhooks: routines live in the app library. Webhook addresses and keys are kept in Android secure storage and leave the phone only when the routine itself calls the service you set up.
  • Fingerprint and passcode: a routine can ask for your fingerprint, face or phone passcode before it runs. Android itself checks it; the app only learns "confirmed" or "not confirmed", never the biometric data or the passcode. The option to show the routine in the status bar uses a notification on the device, which is not sent anywhere.
  • Backup: the backup file is created and stored wherever you choose; the app does not send it to any server. It does not include webhook addresses or keys.
  • Preferences (theme, tour seen) stay on the device.

The app needs no account, shows no ads and does not sell data.

What is sent to measure use and fix crashes

The app uses Google Firebase (Analytics and Crashlytics) to learn which features work and where it fails. It sends:

  • Usage events: the kind of operation and its result, for example "wrote a Wi-Fi tag successfully" or "the read failed because the tag moved away". The content of the tag or QR code is never part of these events.
  • Crash and diagnostic reports: the error, the screen, the device model and the Android version.
  • An app identifier on the device, generated by Firebase, which does not identify you by name.
  • Approximate region: Google infers the country and city from the connection's IP address. The IP address is not stored, and the app never uses GPS.

The data travels encrypted and is stored by Google as a service provider, under the Firebase privacy policy. It is not shared with other companies or used for advertising, and the app does not collect the Android advertising ID.

How long

Usage events are kept for up to 2 months and crash reports for up to 90 days. After that, they are deleted automatically. Content saved in the app stays until you delete it or uninstall the app.

Your rights

The data sent to Firebase carries neither your name nor your email, only the random app identifier. That is why it cannot be found from a request: it is deleted on its own within the periods above. Uninstalling the app stops all sending right away.
